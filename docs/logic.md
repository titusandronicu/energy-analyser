## Live state

- The lab pushes every 5 minutes. A snapshot older than **15 minutes** is shown as stale with its age ("5 min", "2 godz.", "3 dni"), never hidden; older than **2 hours** it is a problem (the lab has most likely stopped pushing). See [Status colours and data periods](#status-colours-and-data-periods).
- Flows below **50 W** count as noise and show as "0,0 kW" without a direction word.
- Sign conventions: grid positive = buying from the grid, negative = selling; battery positive = discharging, negative = charging. The app shows the value unsigned with a direction word.
- **System balance ("Bilans systemu").** PV production minus home load, both in watts from the snapshot's `state` (`pv_w` − `home_load_w`), computed in `balanceOf` in `src/lib/services/live-state.ts`; it is not a field the lab sends. Positive is a surplus ("nadwyżka", printed with `+`), negative a shortfall ("niedobór", printed with a real minus). A difference under the **50 W** floor (`MIN_FLOW_W`, the same noise floor as above) is "zbilansowany" and prints "0,0 kW" with no sign. It is computed from the unrounded watts, so it can differ by 0,1 kW from the difference of the two figures shown beside it (3140 W and 860 W show as "3,1 kW" and "0,9 kW" but balance to "+2,3 kW"). A missing or non-numeric reading on either side gives "—", never zero. It is not rated: no tone, no verdict, no colour beyond the primary accent. It shows under the hub of the diagram, in the "Odczyty" view and in the glossary ("Bilans systemu"); on a stale snapshot it is still computed and shown, dimmed to `opacity-70`.

## Live state: node verdicts

The flow diagram on the live card rates three of its four nodes (battery, PV, home) as good, watch or problem, with a chip carrying an icon and the word, so colour is never the only signal. Grid is not rated: import and export are neither good nor bad, only a direction. The neutral "Bez oceny" chip the grid node shows is a display constant of the component that keeps the four nodes alike, not a rating. Every rule is a named constant in `src/lib/services/live-state.ts`; exactly on a line is always the milder status, and the verdicts describe, they never advise.

| Node    | Compares                                                                      | good                                  | watch               | problem       |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| Battery | The charge level now, as the whole percent the label shows                    | **30%** and above (100% is never bad) | **10** to under 30% | under **10%** |
| PV      | Today's kWh so far against the day's forecast × the share expected by capture | **85%** of expected and above         | **60** to under 85% | under **60%** |
| Home    | Today's kWh so far against the daily norm × hours elapsed ÷ 24                | up to **+15%** (lower is never bad)   | over +15%           | over **+40%** |
| Grid    | Nothing                                                                       | not rated                             | not rated           | not rated     |

- **Battery:** the verdict and the icon band read the same whole percent as the label ("29,9" shows as 30% and is rated good), so the number, the icon and the chip never disagree at a line. "Wysoki poziom" from 80%, "w normie" below it, "niski poziom" for watch, "prawie pusta" for problem.
- **PV, from 15:00.** Before **15:00** Warsaw time at capture nothing is rated ("za wcześnie"). From then the expected share of the day's forecast rises linearly from the month's 15:00 share to 1.0 at the hour the day is done, and stays 1.0 afterwards; the reading is today's PV total in the same snapshot divided by the forecast in today's `daily_history` entry × that share. The table gives `[share at 15:00, hour done]` per month:

  | Jan       | Feb       | Mar       | Apr       | May       | Jun       | Jul       | Aug       | Sep       | Oct       | Nov       | Dec       |
  | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- |
  | 0.98/15.3 | 0.92/16.1 | 0.89/16.8 | 0.76/18.5 | 0.75/19.2 | 0.73/19.7 | 0.73/19.6 | 0.75/18.9 | 0.80/17.9 | 0.86/16.9 | 0.99/15.1 | 1.00/14.8 |

  The values are **estimated from clear-sky solar geometry** (52.2 N 21.0 E, flat array, mid-month), not measured; revisit after a year of lab data. Example: 16:10 in September is 0.80 + 0.20 × (16.17 − 15) ÷ (17.9 − 15) ≈ 0.88 of the forecast. A single constant would mislabel seasons, because the share produced by 15:00 runs from 0.73 in June and July to 1.00 in December.

- **Home, from 06:00.** Before **06:00** nothing is rated ("za wcześnie"). From then today's consumption in the daily history is compared with the usage card's norm (the median of the baseline days, [Usage insight](#usage-insight-is-recent-usage-normal), the same seasonal-else-fallback selection) multiplied by the hours elapsed ÷ 24 at capture. The bands are the usage card's **+15%** and **+40%**. The pro-rating is linear, which is crude for a house with a heat pump; the chip states what it compares ("+5% wobec normy").
- **Home needs a current daily row.** The daily history is written independently of the snapshot, and a push may omit it, so an earlier push's partial total can sit next to a newer snapshot. Consumption is therefore rated only when today's row (`daily_energy.captured_at`) was captured at most **15 minutes** before the snapshot (`DAILY_ROW_MAX_LAG_MS`, exactly 15 minutes is fresh; a row newer than the snapshot is fine). The lab pushes every 5 minutes, so a bigger gap means the total comes from an earlier reading and would be read as consumption up to a time it does not cover. PV is not affected: its total comes from the snapshot and the forecast is the day's morning value.
- **Capture-time basis.** PV and consumption are judged at the snapshot's `captured_at` in Europe/Warsaw (its hour, month and calendar day), because "today so far" totals end at capture; the day's row is the capture's own Warsaw day. Staleness is judged against now.
- **Not rated ("bez oceny"), with the reason as the detail word:** "dane nieaktualne" (snapshot older than 15 minutes, for all three); "za wcześnie" (PV before 15:00, home before 06:00); "brak prognozy" (no PV forecast for today in the daily history); "brak odczytu" (no battery percentage, no PV total in the snapshot, or no consumption in today's row); "brak danych historii" (no daily history at all, for example no Supabase client); "historia niedostępna" (loading the daily history failed, so the reading is not rated); "historia nieaktualna" (home only: today's daily row was captured more than **15 minutes** before the snapshot); "brak czasu historii" (home only: the row's capture time is unknown, for example when it failed to load or the column grant is not applied yet); "za mało danych" (home only: fewer than **8** earlier days with a consumption value, that is yesterday or the newest day within 7 days plus at least 7 baseline days). A missing reading is never rated as zero.
- **Figures at a line.** Percentages at an edge show one decimal (84,9% is watch, 85,0% is good; +15,0% is good, +15,1% is watch), as the usage card does, so the badge and the number agree. The PV chip states its share of the expected production ("94% oczekiwanego").
- **Motion gate.** A flow moves only when the snapshot is fresh (not older than 15 minutes), the reading is at least **50 W** in either direction and the user has not paused motion. Arrowheads always show direction (an inactive flow draws a dotted line without one) and stay when paused; with reduced motion requested by the system nothing moves at all. A degraded but fresh snapshot still moves. Pausing and the view choice (diagram or readings) are stored in the browser's `localStorage` only, never any reading.
- **Two words for "cannot rate".** The chip on a node says "bez oceny", while the status badge in a card header says "za mało danych" (`TONE_WORD`); both are the neutral grey tone, and the chip is shorter because it also carries the reason.

## Daily sparklines

Small line charts of the daily totals the page already loads (the same 400 days of `daily_energy` rows as the usage insight; no extra query). `src/lib/services/daily-series.ts` turns rows into a calendar-indexed series and `src/lib/sparkline.ts` turns a series into path data and a label; the chart is server-rendered SVG with no script and no animation.

- **Windows.** The live card's KPI row shows the previous **14** days (`KPI_SERIES_DAYS`) of PV production, grid purchase and grid export. The series ends on the day before the capture's Warsaw calendar day, so a snapshot captured on an earlier day than now still ends the day before its own capture day. The "Zużycie wczoraj" rows show **7** days (`USAGE_SERIES_DAYS`) of consumption and grid purchase ending on the compared day, so the last point is the value shown beside it, also when the compared day is older than yesterday.
- **Today is never plotted.** Today's row is a partial that each push replaces, and a running total next to complete days would read as a collapse. The KPI values beside the charts are today's partial totals; a footnote says the charts are "poprzednie 14 dni, bez dzisiejszego".
- **Gaps, never zeros.** Each slot is one calendar day. A day with no row, a null total, a non-finite total or a negative total is a gap: the line breaks there, and a single day between two gaps is drawn as a dot. A trailing gap leaves the right end empty. Rows outside the window are ignored.
- **Minimum:** fewer than **3** days with a value (`MIN_SPARKLINE_POINTS`) draws no chart; the card says "za mało dni". Two points make a line that reads as a trend, three is the least that shows a shape.
- **Two different empty states.** "historia niedostępna" means loading the daily history failed (rows `null`, so the live view carries no series); it is never worded as "za mało dni", so a database error does not read as a short history. Empty or thin history is "za mało dni". The usage card has no unavailable variant: its view exists only when the rows loaded.
- **Scale.** Each series is scaled on its own from its minimum (bottom) to its maximum (top), so a nearly flat series looks lively and two charts are not comparable by height; the label carries the numbers. A **flat** series (all present values equal) is not scaled: all zero is drawn on the baseline, any other equal value at mid-height, so a counter that reads zero every day never looks like a steady mid-scale trend. Every flat chart also shows a visible caption under it ("bez zmian, 0,0 kWh", "bez zmian, 3,0 kWh"), because a flat line carries no scale.
- **Label (accessible name).** States the subject, the window, the minimum, the maximum and the last available value, never a trend, arrow or percentage: "Produkcja z paneli, ostatnie 14 dni: od 8,1 do 25,3 kWh, ostatnia dostępna wartość 24,7 kWh, dane z 12 z 14 dni" (the last clause only when some days are gaps; a flat series reads "bez zmian, 3,0 kWh w każdym dniu z danymi"). The KPI window is "ostatnie 14 dni"; the usage window is "7 dni do <dayLabel>" with the compared day's own label ("7 dni do 28 września"), because that series ends on the compared day, not on today.
- **Export series.** The "Sprzedane do sieci dziś" chart uses the inverter's export counter, like the value beside it, and inherits its under-reading (94,7 kWh against PGE's 342 for August, see [Bill forecast](#bill-forecast-lab--app)). It makes no trend claim, and a flat-zero series is the likeliest outcome, hence the caption above.
- **No "Z PV wykorzystane".** Self-consumption would be PV minus export, which rests on the same under-reading counter and would overstate it, so the usage card does not show it. Revisit when the lab computes self-consumption.

## Usage insight: is recent usage normal?

The app compares the most recent complete day's consumption with a baseline and says _normal_, _above_ or _below_.

1. **Compared day:** yesterday, or the newest day with a consumption value within the last **7 days** if yesterday is missing. Today is never compared (it is not over).
2. **Seasonal baseline first:** all days within **±14 days** of the same date in earlier years (up to one year back, since the app loads **400 days** of history). It is used when it has at least **20 days** with data.
3. **Fallback:** otherwise the previous **30 days**, used when at least **7** of them have data, and the card says plainly that the fallback is in use.
4. **Not enough data:** with fewer than 7 fallback days, or no day with consumption in the last 7 days, no verdict is shown; the card says what is missing.
5. **Norm:** the **median** of the baseline days' consumption (for an even count, the mean of the two middle days). Grid purchase uses the median of the baseline days that have one; a median of 0 shows "—" instead of a change.
6. **Verdict:** more than **15%** above or below the norm is _above_ / _below_; exactly ±15% is still _normal_. More than **40%** above is _far above_; exactly +40% is still _above_.
7. The card names the baseline days it used ("mediana z 18 dni: 27 sierpnia – 25 września").
8. **Meaning of the day ("Co to znaczy?"):** the day's kWh, the norm in kWh, and four ranges whose edges are the verdict thresholds: _niskie_ below 0.85 × norm, _w normie_ 0.85–1.15 ×, _wysokie_ 1.15–1.40 ×, _bardzo wysokie_ above 1.40 ×. The range marked "ten dzień" is always the one the badge shows; a day whose one-decimal kWh would print as an edge it is not on (34,52 against the edge 34,5) shows two decimals, as the percentage does at ±15% and +40% ("+40,0%" is still _wysokie_, "+40,1%" is _bardzo wysokie_). A zero norm draws no ranges and the card shows the glossary instead.
9. **Comparison with a typical house:** one line with the estimated daily use of a ~140 m² Polish house with an air-source heat pump in the compared day's month (29, 28, 24, 20, 15, 11, 10, 11, 13, 18, 24, 28 kWh for January–December; `src/lib/format/reference-usage.ts`). It is context only and never enters the norm or the verdict. The figures are an estimate, not a measured statistic: about 7,000 kWh a year from space heat of 55–80 kWh/m² (PORT PC, Q3 2024) at a seasonal COP of 3.0–3.4 (Fraunhofer ISE field test, 2025), heat-pump hot water (about 1,150 kWh) and appliances (about 2,800 kWh, GUS 2021), spread over the months with Enea's G11 standard profile (2025) and Eurostat heating degree-days for Poland (2015–2025); the monthly table is in `context/archive/2026-09-26-usage-norm-scale/research.md`.

Why the median: the mean was a default, never a decision. On the real data (2026-08-26 to 2026-09-25) the two disagreed on 3 of 19 days, each time because a few inconsistent early rows (2026-07-26 to 2026-08-03) pulled the mean down; one unusual day moves a median far less. Why the typical house stays out of the norm: this house used about 36 kWh a day in September 2026 against about 13 kWh for the estimate, so blending the estimate in would mark ordinary days as above the norm. The norm corrects itself over time from the house's own days: the 30-day window moves daily, and the seasonal baseline takes over once a year of history exists.

Why seasonal: a flat recent average mislabels normal seasonal change (winter heating, summer air conditioning) as anomalies. The app's history starts on 2026-07-16, like the lab's (the F-03 backfill added 2026-07-16 to 2026-07-25 on 2026-09-30; 2026-07-20 has no totals), so the fallback is what runs until about July 2027, when a year of history exists.

## Godziny zużycia

The hourly rules behind the "Godziny zużycia" card: how much the house draws from the grid at night, and which hours and days had the highest and lowest house use. The lab sends one row per clock hour (`hourly_history`, stored in `hourly_energy`, kept **35 days**); the rules run in `src/lib/services/hourly-usage.ts`, with the Warsaw hour helpers in `src/lib/format/warsaw-time.ts`.

- **Window:** the last **35** Warsaw days (`HOURLY_HISTORY_DAYS`), from Warsaw midnight 35 days before today. An hour that has not ended yet is ignored. The card names the days the complete hours come from ("19 dni: 1–19 sierpnia", the usual [period text](#status-colours-and-data-periods)) and states the completeness rule in words.
- **Hours:** keyed by the UTC instant they start at and labelled in Europe/Warsaw ("9 sierpnia", "niedziela", "20:00–21:00"). Both hours of the autumn change read "02:00–03:00".
- **Complete hour:** at least **10** of its 12 five-minute readings (`MIN_HOUR_SAMPLES`), with both house use and net grid present. Anything else is a gap and is left out of every figure; only complete hours are ranked.
- **Complete day:** every clock hour of the Warsaw date is present and complete: **24** hours, **23** on the spring change (no 02:00) and **25** on the autumn change (02:00 twice). Only complete days are ranked; a day's total is the sum of its hours' house use.
- **Sanity bound (contract):** one hour's `load_kwh` and `pv_kwh` must be 0–50 kWh and `grid_net_kwh` within ±50 kWh (`MAX_HOURLY_KWH`); the house peaked at about 8 kWh in an hour, so anything beyond is a sensor glitch, and a push carrying one is refused whole (422).
- **Grid draw:** per hour `max(grid_net_kwh, 0)`, the net hourly import, counted the way PGE bills it after hourly balancing; export is never subtracted from another hour. The figure is the inverter's, read by its faulty grid sensor, so it is not PGE's figure ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)).
- **Night:** 22:00 to 06:00 local time (`NIGHT_START_HOUR`, `NIGHT_END_HOUR`), named by both dates: "noc z 1 na 2 sierpnia", "noc z 31 lipca na 1 sierpnia". That is **8** clock hours, **7** across the spring change and **9** across the autumn change. A night is complete when all its hours are present and complete, and counts only once its 06:00 end has passed. Its grid draw is the sum of its hours' grid draw, so an exporting night hour adds 0, not a negative amount.
- **Last night:** the most recent complete night among the last **3** that have ended (`LAST_NIGHT_LOOKBACK`), shown by its name. When none of the three is complete the card says "niepełne dane za ostatnie noce" and shows no figure.
- **Night average:** the mean grid draw of every complete night in the window, with how many nights it rests on.
- **Hour rankings:** the **5** highest and **5** lowest complete hours by house use (`RANKED_HOURS`), each with its date, weekday, clock hour, house use and grid draw. They need at least **1** complete day (`MIN_RANKED_HOUR_DAYS`); below that the card says "za mało danych: brak pełnego dnia". **Suspect hours stay off the lowest list:** an hour whose grid draw exceeds its house use by more than **0.5 kWh** (`SUSPECT_GRID_MARGIN_KWH`, `isSuspectLowHour`) is not a quiet hour but a load-reading drop-out or the battery charging from the grid. It is still ranked among the highest, counted in its day's total and in the night draw; only the lowest list leaves it out. Found in production on 2026-09-30: 7 of 547 complete hours showed about 0.2 kWh of house use against about 1.9 kWh from the grid, where a normal night hour uses about 1.0 kWh, and all 7 had filled the lowest list.
- **Day rankings:** the **3** highest and **3** lowest complete days by house use (`RANKED_DAYS`), each with its date, weekday and total. They need at least **7** complete days (`MIN_RANKED_DAYS`); below that the card says "za mało dni: N z 7".
- **Ties** go to the more recent hour or day, in both the highest and the lowest list.
- **No data:** no hour in the window is "brak danych godzinowych"; rows without a single complete hour say so after the same words. A failure to load is the card's load error, as for every card.
- **The card** (`src/components/HourlyUsageCard.astro`) sits full width below the card grid, inside the main landmark, and loads on its own, so a failure only shows this card's load error. It shows last night's grid draw beside the night average, the hour lists ("Najwyższe" / "Najniższe", house use first and grid draw beside it), the day lists with their weekday, the window and the completeness rule in words, and the sensor caveat with the ranking rule below. It is server-rendered with no client JavaScript and no chart.
- **Badge:** the card rates nothing, so it has no good or bad verdict. With data, the badge is the neutral (grey) tone and says what the figures rest on: "Pełne dane z N dni" ("z 1 dnia" for one, "Dane bez pełnego dnia" when there is none). The empty state keeps "Za mało danych" with its reason below, and a load failure is "Problem · nie udało się wczytać".
- **Grid draw and house use carry the sensor caveat.** Both hourly figures come from the inverter's grid sensor, which has measured wrongly for the whole history, and house use is derived from the same sensor ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)). The card shows the shared caveat (`SENSOR_CAVEAT`) followed by its ranking rule: hours and days are ranked by house use, and suspect hours stay off the lowest list. It gives no percentage for the error: by the hourly fit a fixed offset of about 0.70 kWh an hour dominates at low load, so any percentage depends on the load. The **11.8%** is kept only as a historical measurement: over the five nights 26–31 August 2026 (22:00–06:00) the inverter read 11.8% above PGE's hourly-balanced import (45.4 against 40.6 kWh; single nights +5% to +21%), checked after the 2026-09-30 backfill (`context/changes/grid-export-mismatch/frame.md`). It is not a correction and the card does not show it.

## Today's recommendation

- The lab's deterministic rules compute a facts bundle; the stronger LLM narrates it in Polish without adding numbers. The app shows the narration with the forecast and the facts it is based on.
- The recommendation is **stale** when it was generated more than **2 hours** ago (the lab narrates about hourly, so two missed runs) or before the start of today.
- **Always visible:** the status badge and generation time, the first block of the advice (below), the lab's findings (up to five), and the forecast. Behind "Pokaż szczegóły" sit the rest of the advice, "Co to znaczy i co sprawdzić" (each finding's meaning and suggested check, for findings that have one), "Pozostałe ustalenia" (findings beyond the fifth) and the model label.
- **Findings and severity:** the lab's `local_findings` are free-form, so the mapper reads only `title`, `fact`, `meaning`, `suggested_check` and `severity`, and guards each one. Severity maps to a chip with its own word: `warn` → "Warto sprawdzić" (amber), `ok` → "Dobrze" (green), `info` → "Informacja" (grey), anything else or missing → "Bez oceny" (grey). No category is guessed from the wording; a lab `kind` field would be a separate change.
- **Where the findings come from:** they are fixed rules in homelab-2 `infra/compose/energy-app/scripts/build-energy-agent-briefing.py` (`build_findings`), not LLM output; the app shows them as sent. Since 2026-09-30 (`lab-findings-cleanup`): "Rozjazd" compares a closed month's computed bill with that month's PGE invoice (the bill forecast's `closed_month_check`) and appears only when the check fails; there is no high-import rule while the grid sensor is faulty; an overdue PGE payment appears only 7 or more days past the due date, as information with neutral wording; every text has Polish diacritics and states facts, not advice.
- **Stale cards stay neutral:** when the recommendation is stale (over 2 hours old or from an earlier day), every finding chip keeps its word but is grey, so an old card never claims freshness through colour.
- **Order and cap:** findings sort by severity: warnings first, then information and unrated together, then ok; the lab's own order holds within each rank. The first **5** (`FINDINGS_VISIBLE_MAX`) show in the card; the rest go behind the disclosure in the same order. A finding with neither a title nor a fact is dropped.
- **Guards on finding text:** each text field is trimmed; a blank or non-string value counts as missing; a value over **500** characters (`FINDING_TEXT_MAX_CHARS`) is cut to its first 499 plus "…". The ingest contract already caps strings at 500, so a valid row never reaches the cut.
- **First block of the advice:** the always-visible lead is every leading heading (a `#` line or a bold-only line) plus the first block after them, a paragraph or a whole list. If that block is a one-line paragraph ending in ":" ("Zalecenia na dziś:") and a list follows, the list joins the lead, so the actionable items never hide behind their own intro; with no list after it the intro stands alone (`splitAdviceLead`). The lead has no length cap and the text is never edited.
- **Forecast in the card:** the forecast sits inside the recommendation card, under the findings, not in a card of its own. The forecast figures name their days: "dziś" and "jutro" are the day the advice was generated and the day after. Advice from an earlier day (`isFromEarlierDay`) gets date-only labels ("28 września"), because its "today" and "tomorrow" are other days than the reader's.
- **Motion:** only a current recommendation (status good, `isCurrent`) animates, and only its data: each finding fades in and rises a few pixels, staggered by 60 ms per item, the forecast section fades in once, and the "Pokaż szczegóły" content fades in with a turning chevron. The advice text never moves. Watch and problem cards, and the empty and failed states, do not move at all. Every animation is CSS-only, plays once, and is switched off by `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- Forecast certainty is always shown as not known yet ("jeszcze nie wiadomo — prognozy zbierane od 27 września"); the lab's optional `confidence` is ignored because it never states its basis. **Planned (S-11):** certainty computed from past forecast accuracy and Solcast's low/high range. Accuracy counts days from **2026-09-27** (`FORECAST_HISTORY_START`): the stored forecast for 2026-09-26 came from Forecast.Solar, which overshoots.

## Status colours and data periods

Every card shows a status badge under its heading: a colour **and** a word, so the text alone carries the meaning.

| Tone         | Colour | Word            |
| ------------ | ------ | --------------- |
| good         | green  | dobrze          |
| watch        | amber  | warto sprawdzić |
| problem      | red    | problem         |
| insufficient | grey   | za mało danych  |

The badge reads "<word> · <detail>", e.g. "Warto sprawdzić · dane sprzed 40 min". A card that rates nothing ([Godziny zużycia](#godziny-zużycia)) keeps the grey tone and replaces the word with what its figures rest on. Exactly at a line is always the milder status. A card whose data failed to load shows "Problem · nie udało się wczytać".

- **Live state:** good ("aktualne") up to **15 minutes** old; watch ("dane sprzed …") over 15 minutes; problem ("brak nowych danych od …") over **2 hours**. A fresh snapshot the lab marks degraded is watch ("niepełne dane z Home Assistant"); staleness wins over degraded. Nothing received yet is insufficient.
- **Usage insight:** normal or below the norm is good ("w normie" / "poniżej normy"); more than **15%** above is watch ("powyżej normy"); more than **40%** above is problem ("dużo powyżej normy"). The grid-purchase figure shows its change against the norm but is not rated (day and month ratings are in [Ocena dni i miesięcy](#ocena-dni-i-miesięcy)).
- **Recommendation:** good ("aktualna") when generated today within **2 hours**; watch ("sprzed …") when from today but older; problem ("z <day> — dotyczy innego dnia") when generated before today. Nothing received yet is insufficient. In the history calendar a past day's advice is not judged against today: its badge is neutral and names its generation time ([Historia](#historia-kalendarz)).
- **Bill forecast:** the badge is the verdict against the last real invoice — good ("nie więcej niż ostatni rachunek (…)"), watch ("do 20% powyżej …"), problem ("ponad 20% powyżej …"); insufficient when there is no closed invoice to compare with. Every refusal path (the lab's three `no_data` reasons, a calculation older than **30 minutes** or dated in the future, an unreadable, reversed, self-contradicting or implausible amount, fewer than **7** complete days) shows its reason and no number. A forecast for another month keeps its figure and turns problem, naming the month it covers. The card's own confidence badge is the lab's `confidence`, and it names the reference month whenever that month lags.
- **Forecast certainty:** always insufficient, "jeszcze nie wiadomo", until S-11 computes it from forecast accuracy.
- **Not enough data:** the usage card says what it needs: "brak zużycia z ostatnich 7 dni", or "potrzeba co najmniej 7 dni z ostatnich 30, jest <n>". No verdict is guessed from too little data.
- **Period text:** every derived figure says which days it rests on, as "<count> <dzień|dni>: <first> – <last>", e.g. "1 dzień: 24 września", "18 dni: 27 sierpnia – 25 września", "2 dni: 23–24 września". The range runs from the first to the last day used even when days in between are missing. The year is shown on both ends when the range spans two years, and once when the whole range is in an earlier year ("20 dni: 10–29 września 2025"). Live "today" totals say "dziś od północy do <HH:MM>" (or the capture's date when it is from an earlier day).
- **Terms:** each card has a folded "Co to znaczy?" box explaining its technical terms in one sentence each (`src/lib/format/glossary.ts`). The live card's list includes "Bilans systemu": panels minus the home's use right now, plus is a surplus that goes to the battery or the grid, minus is a shortfall the home takes from the battery or the grid.
- **Readability:** text keeps at least **4.5:1** contrast against its surface (WCAG 1.4.3), through the palette tokens in `src/styles/global.css` and no colour literal in components: status text is `text-tone-good`, `text-tone-watch`, `text-tone-problem` or `text-tone-neutral` on its own `-surface` tint (10% of the tone over `--card`), and the small grey notes are `text-muted-foreground` (`#a9b1c6`), never a fade of it. Measured on the rendered dev page: chip and badge text at least **7,2:1** (the lowest is the problem tone), muted text **6,4:1 or higher** on the card, the inner surface and the inset panel, and the balance value on a stale snapshot, dimmed to `opacity-70`, about **4,6:1** (a fade to `opacity-45` measured 2,6:1 and was rejected). The dimmed connectors of a stale snapshot use `opacity-65` on the source colour: **3,1:1** (grid), **4,6:1** (battery), **5,3:1** (PV) and **6,1:1** (home) on the stage, so every arrowhead meets the 3:1 graphics guideline (`opacity-45` gave 2,1 to 3,6:1). Borders and hairlines are decorative (about 1,2 to 1,4:1 against the card): meaning never rests on them, only on the word, icon or value beside them. The "Co to znaczy?" and "Na podstawie" toggles are at least 24 px tall for a thumb (WCAG 2.5.8).
- **Advice from an earlier day:** the recommendation view says so explicitly (`isFromEarlierDay`); the card names the forecast days by date instead of "dziś"/"jutro" from that flag, not from the badge colour.

### Grid sensor caveat

The inverter's grid sensor has measured wrongly since the history began (2026-07-16), and the inverter derives house use from the same sensor: from 2026-08-04 its power balance `pv + grid + battery − load` closes on every sample with a +130–190 W residual (losses), while the true night load stays at about 0.7–0.8 kW and the inverter's swings between 0.48 and 1.79 kW. So every grid import, grid export and house-use figure in the app is uncertain for the whole history. Nothing is corrected numerically (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-09-30).

- **What was measured** (PGE's hourly meter data against the inverter, 16 July – 31 August 2026, import-only hours, aggregates only): before the change the inverter's hourly net fits about −0.53 × PGE's net − 0.70 kWh (R² 0.99), after it about +0.53 × PGE's net + 0.70 kWh (R² 0.90). July's import read 151 kWh against PGE's hourly-balanced ~350 kWh; in August the inverter's net import was about 260 kWh above PGE's, and its export read 95 against 342 kWh. The likeliest cause, not verified, is a current sensor paired with the wrong phase; the installer checks it on site, outside the app.
- **Direction change:** at about 16:00 on 2026-08-03 (`SENSOR_CHANGE_DAY`, a mixed day) the sensor's direction changed, and 2026-08-04 (`SENSOR_DIRECTION_CHANGED_ON`) is the first whole day after it. Before it the sensor under-reads grid import; from it the sensor over-reads import, mostly during the day, and under-reads export. "Co to znaczy?" (`SENSOR_EXPLANATION`) says the sensor under-read import until the afternoon of 3 August and over-reads it from 4 August; elsewhere the app names 4 August 2026 only as the day the sensor's direction changed, never as the start of the error.
- **Where it is shown:** one line (`SENSOR_CAVEAT`) on the live card, the usage card, the "Godziny zużycia" card and the bill forecast card (its estimate is built from the inverter's month import); the history page's chart captions and a "Co to znaczy?" entry (`SENSOR_TERM`, `SENSOR_EXPLANATION`). The usage card adds `SENSOR_CHANGE_BASELINE_NOTE` when its baseline starts before 2026-08-04 and the compared day is on or after it (`baseline.crossesSensorChange`), whether the baseline straddles the change or lies wholly before it; the note says the norm and the compared day lie on different sides of 4 August 2026. The ratings leave the sensor-change window unrated ([Ocena dni i miesięcy](#ocena-dni-i-miesięcy)).
- **One source:** the dates, the texts and `crossesSensorChange(first, last)` (true when `first < 2026-08-04 ≤ last`) live in `src/lib/services/grid-sensor.ts`, a leaf module that the calendar, the ratings and the cards all read.

## Historia (kalendarz)

The history page (`/dashboard/history`, S-15) shows a day, a month or a quarter of the daily totals the lab pushes. The rules live in `src/lib/calendar/period.ts` (periods and links), `src/lib/services/calendar-view.ts` (every figure and sentence) and `src/lib/bars.ts` (the bar charts); the page only renders them.

- **Periods in the URL:** `?day=2026-09-14`, `?month=2026-09` or `?quarter=2026-Q3`, exactly one of them. A malformed or combined parameter, or a period wholly before **2026-07-16** (`HISTORY_START`, the first day the app holds) or after today, redirects to `/dashboard/history`. Previous/next and every day or month link stop at those two ends, so no link redirects.
- **Default month:** with no parameter the page opens on the current month once it has at least **7** complete days, otherwise on the previous month, never before July 2026. So the first week of a month opens on the month before, with totals and charts, and the current month is one "next" away.
- **Complete day:** a day before today (Europe/Warsaw) whose PV production, house use and grid import are all present (`pv_kwh`, `load_kwh`, `grid_import_kwh` not null). Today is never complete: its day view says the day is still going and marks its figures as running totals. Grid export is not part of the rule.
- **Minimum:** a month's or a quarter's totals need at least **7** complete days (`MIN_RANKED_DAYS`, the same minimum as the hourly rankings); below it the view says "za mało danych: N z 7 potrzebnych pełnych dni" and shows no sum.
- **Sums with their day count:** totals are sums over the complete days only, shown with "Pełne dane: N z M dni" (M is the period's calendar days) and the period text of the days used ("18 dni: 1–29 września"). An unfinished month or quarter says so ("Miesiąc jeszcze trwa — liczą się tylko pełne dni, bez szacowania całości") and is **never extrapolated** to the whole period. July 2026 and Q3 2026 also say the data starts on 16 July.
- **Missing days are gaps:** a day with no row ("brak danych") or with a missing total ("dane niepełne") is left empty: no bar in the charts, no part in the sums, never a zero or an estimate. Today and later days are gaps in the charts too. The month grid names each day's state in words (and by border style), and marks days that have a recommendation.
- **Charts:** the month has daily PV bars with a house-use line and daily grid-import bars; the quarter has its months side by side (PV, house use, grid import), a month under the 7-day minimum being an empty slot. Both captions say that grid import and house use are uncertain for the whole history ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)). Bars start at zero; fewer than **3** slots with a value draw "za mało dni" (in the quarter "za mało miesięcy z danymi"). There is no hourly chart: the app keeps hours for 35 days only.
- **Forecast against actual:** only days from **2026-09-27** (`FORECAST_HISTORY_START`) that are complete and carry a stored forecast count. A month needs **7** such days, otherwise "za mało danych — prognozy zbierane od 27 września". A day before 2026-09-27 says "prognoza nie była jeszcze zbierana", a later day without one "brak prognozy".
- **The day's recommendation:** the first recommendation generated at or after **06:00** Warsaw time, because the morning one set the day's battery plan. With only earlier ones, the day's first is shown, marked "wygenerowana przed 6:00". The day's other recommendations sit in a folded "Pozostałe (N)" in time order. A past day's advice is shown as a record of that day: a neutral badge with its generation time ("z 27 września, 14:00"), never "dotyczy innego dnia", and its findings keep their own colours.
- **Recommendation markers:** the month reads only generation times, at most **1000** (PostgREST's row cap); when it gets exactly 1000 the grid says the markers may be incomplete.
- **Left out:** grid export (the same faulty sensor under-reads it, and it reads 0 from about mid-August 2026 while PGE recorded real export) and PGE figures (a privacy decision first). Grid import and house use are shown with the whole-history sensor caveat ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)); 4 August 2026 appears only as the day the sensor's direction changed. "Co to znaczy?" explains all of this.
- **Ratings:** a completed day and a completed month carry a rating, described in [Ocena dni i miesięcy](#ocena-dni-i-miesięcy). A day or month view reads its daily rows from **14** days before its first day, for the rating's norm; the totals, grid, charts and forecast still use only the period's own days.

## Ocena dni i miesięcy

A completed day and a completed month in the history calendar are rated good, neutral or bad (S-17, FR-022). The rules are in `src/lib/services/period-rating.ts`, where every constant is named; the calendar only renders them. A rating describes and never advises.

- **Self-sufficiency:** `1 − grid import ÷ house use` in percent, for a complete day (the calendar's rule, `src/lib/services/complete-day.ts`) with house use above zero. A day with use **0** is not rated ("dom nie zużył tego dnia prądu, więc nie ma czego oceniać"). A day whose import exceeds its use is not rated either (next point), so the value stays within **0–100%** on its own; the code's clamp only guards against float error.
- **Consistency:** a day whose grid import is larger than its house use (a few of the early rows in late July and early August 2026, roadmap open question 7) reads "Poza oceną" with both figures and a neutral cause ("np. ładowanie baterii z sieci albo błąd licznika"), is not rated and never enters a norm. The wording doesn't call the day an error, since charging the battery from the grid is a legitimate cause.
- **Norm:** the median self-sufficiency of the qualifying days (complete, consistent, use above zero) in the **14** days before the rated day (`RATING_WINDOW_DAYS`), needing at least **7** of them (`RATING_MIN_DAYS`, the calendar's `MIN_RANKED_DAYS`). Fewer reads "Za mało danych: N z 7". The norm is always this recent one and the basis says so ("ostatnie dni, bo z tej pory roku jest za mało danych"): a same-season norm needs a year of history (about July 2027).
- **Bands:** the gap is the day's self-sufficiency minus the norm, in percentage points. More than **+10** points (`RATING_THRESHOLD_POINTS`) is good ("Dobry dzień", green), more than **−10** below is bad ("Słaby dzień", red), and anything between is neutral ("Przeciętny dzień", grey). Exactly ±10 stays neutral, and the gap is printed by the edge rule (`edgePointsLabel`): exactly on the line "10,0 punktu", past it at least "10,1 punktu".
- **Basis:** every rating names its figures, its norm's period and its number of days, e.g. (illustrative figures) "Samowystarczalność 35,0% — 25,0 punktu poniżej normy. Norma: mediana z 12 dni (3–16 marca), ostatnie dni, bo z tej pory roku jest za mało danych."
- **Low sun:** when the day's PV is below **70%** (`LOW_SUN_SHARE`) of the median PV of the same norm days, the rating adds, for example, "Mało słońca: 8,0 kWh z paneli, zwykle 20,0 kWh." Exactly 70% is not low. Self-sufficiency mostly follows the sun, so cloudy days rate lower; the note gives the cause.
- **Sensor-change window:** grid import and house use come from the faulty grid sensor for the whole history, and its error flips sign at the direction change on 2026-08-03 ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)), so a 14-day norm reaching across the change does not rest on comparable days. The mixed day 2026-08-03 (`SENSOR_CHANGE_DAY`) and every day whose 14-day window reaches before 2026-08-04, that is **2026-08-03 to 2026-08-17**, read "Poza oceną" with a neutral basis that names the sensor change: 08-03 says that the direction changed that afternoon, so the day mixes readings from both sides (`SENSOR_CHANGE_DAY_BASIS`), and 08-04 to 08-17 say that their norm would reach days before the change (`SENSOR_CHANGE_RATING_BASIS`). The check runs after the no-use check and before the import-above-use check, so a window day names the sensor rather than a meter error. A norm never mixes days from both sides: 08-03 enters only the norms of 08-04 to 08-17, which are unrated, and a consistent window day still counts in the norms of 08-18 onwards, which lie wholly after the change. 08-01 and 08-02 are rated against pre-change norms when they have enough norm days. "Co to znaczy?" says so.
- **Month:** a completed month is rated by the median of the gaps of its days that were rated good, neutral or bad, with the same ±10 rule and its own words ("Dobry / Przeciętny / Słaby miesiąc"). It needs at least **7** rated days, else "Za mało danych: N z 7" ("Brak danych z tego miesiąca" when none of its days is rated). The basis names the count and the period of the rated days. The month holding the sensor change (August 2026) counts only its days from 2026-08-04 on, so it never mixes days from both sides even when 08-01 and 08-02 are rated as days: in production it rests on 18–30 August (08-31 has no totals). "Co to znaczy?" (`RATING_EXPLANATION`) states this month rule, and that sensor-window days, though "Poza oceną", still count in later norms, unlike days with import above use, which never do.
- **Colours:** green, grey and red. Neutral uses the grey tone with its own word, so it isn't read as "za mało danych"; "Za mało danych", "Poza oceną" and "Bez oceny" are grey too, each with its words. Amber keeps meaning "warto sprawdzić" and is not used.
- **Where:** a rating panel under "Dzień w liczbach" on the day view, and beside the totals on the month view. Not in the month grid, the quarter view or the dashboard.
- **Not rated:** today and future days (no rating panel), missing or incomplete days (their totals already say so), the current month ("miesiąc jeszcze trwa — oceniamy tylko zakończone miesiące", under a grey "Bez oceny" badge), and the quarter.

## Notatki (day notes)

The owner can write a short note on a calendar day ("urlop", "pompa ciepła od dziś"), read it on that day, see which days of a month have one, edit it and delete it (S-19, US-06, FR-025–FR-028). The rules are in `src/lib/services/day-notes.ts`; the database repeats the text rules in `public.day_notes` (`supabase/migrations/20261001072438_day_notes.sql`), and the security model is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#security-model).

- **One note per day:** at most one note per user and day (`unique (user_id, day)` in `day_notes`). Saving a day that already has a note replaces its text; there is no version history and no undo.
- **Length:** **1–500** characters after trimming (`NOTE_MAX_LENGTH`). Blank or whitespace-only text is rejected. The limit is enforced three times: the zod form schema, the database `check` (`char_length(text) <= 500 and btrim(text) <> ''`) and `maxlength="500"` on the textarea. Line breaks are kept; the text is always shown as escaped plain text, never as HTML.
- **Which days:** only days the calendar can open, **2026-07-16** (`HISTORY_START`) through today in Europe/Warsaw (`isOpenable`, applied through `parsePeriod`). Future days and earlier days are rejected; a malformed day too.
- **Where notes show:**
  - **Day view:** a "Notatka" panel right after "Ocena dnia", with the text and "zmieniona {data, godzina}" (Warsaw time). "Dodaj notatkę" or "Edytuj" opens the form (one labelled textarea and "Zapisz") in a native `<details>`; nothing on the page needs JavaScript.
  - **Month view:** a day with a note carries a small corner triangle, a different shape from the round recommendation dot, so it is not told apart by colour alone. Its accessible name gains ", jest notatka" (`HAS_NOTE_WORD`) and the legend lists "jest notatka". If the noted days fail to load, the grid says "Znaczniki notatek nie są pokazane." and the rest of the month still shows.
  - **Not shown:** in the quarter view or on the dashboard.
- **Delete with confirmation:** "Usuń" opens a second `<details>` step that says the note can't be restored, with a "Na pewno usuń" button. Deleting a day that has no note still counts as deleted, so a repeated submit is harmless.
- **After every post** the browser is redirected (303) back to the day with `?note=`, and the panel shows one notice:
  - `saved`: "Notatka zapisana."
  - `deleted`: "Notatka usunięta."
  - `invalid`: "Notatka jest pusta albo dłuższa niż 500 znaków." (also for an unknown intent or a body that isn't a form; when the day itself can't be opened the redirect goes to `/dashboard/history?note=invalid`, and the notice shows above the calendar)
  - `failed`: "Nie udało się zapisać notatki. Spróbuj ponownie." (a database error, Supabase not configured, or a retried save that changed no row; logged on the server)
- **Line breaks count once:** the form normalises CRLF (and a lone CR) to a line feed before trimming and the length check, so a note within the textarea's `maxlength` is never rejected for its line breaks.
- **Expired session (known limitation):** a post with an expired session redirects to sign-in, and the typed text is lost.
- **A note that fails to load** shows a load error in the panel and no form, since the page can't tell whether a note exists.
- **Notes stay notes:** they never change ratings, summaries or recommendations, and nothing sends them to the lab or to an LLM.

## Daily totals (lab → app)

- Every push carries up to the last **35 days** of per-day totals (PV production, consumption, grid import, grid export, PV forecast); the contract allows at most **62**. Today's entry is partial and replaced by each later push.
- A past day counts only when its counters were read correctly (`counters_ok`) and the data covers the day to its end; incomplete days are sent with empty totals rather than wrong ones.
- The earliest days do not always balance. The early PV and export counters are off, so on 2026-07-18, 07-21, 07-23 and 07-24 PV + import and house use + export differ by 8–32 kWh, and stored days of the same period (such as 07-26 and 07-27) show the same gap. They are kept as sent, under the rules above (owner's decision, 2026-09-30, `history-backfill`; `docs/decisions.md`). The imbalance plausibly fits the same grid-sensor fault, which under-read import until 3 August and feeds the derived house use ([Grid sensor caveat](#grid-sensor-caveat)); this is not verified.
- The day's PV forecast is the first forecast reading at or after **06:00** local time.
- Forecast source since 2026-09-26: **Solcast** (forecast plus low/high estimates, recorded in the lab's history). Forecast.Solar is kept only for comparison; it overshot real production on this flat array. Its today/tomorrow values are recorded in the lab's history once homelab-2 #28 is installed, so the two sources can be compared.

## Bill forecast (lab → app)

The lab has computed this since 2026-09-27 and writes it with every 5-minute refresh; the Telegram bot and the lab page read it. Since 2026-09-28 the app accepts it as the optional `bill_forecast` section and shows it on the dashboard as the "Prognoza rachunku" card (S-07); the lab starts sending it once that version of the app is deployed, as [Changing the contract](ingest/README.md#changing-the-contract) requires. The rules below run in the lab; [what the app does with the figure](#what-the-app-shows-s-07) is at the end of this section.

The house is a prosumer on Poland's older settlement scheme, **net-metering** ("opust"). Energy sent to the grid is not sold for money: it is banked. For every kWh fed in, **0.8** kWh may later be taken back without paying the energy charge, and credit left unused carries over to the following months. So the invoice is not "everything you took × the price". It is "everything you took, minus the credit you had banked, × the price", plus the fixed monthly fees, which are due whatever the meter says.

- **Imported energy** for the month = the mean grid import of the month's complete days so far × the number of days in the month. A day counts only under the same completeness rule as [Daily totals](#daily-totals-lab--app), and today is never counted. A day reading above **200 kWh** is dropped as a counter glitch.
- **Credit** = 0.8 × the export ratio × the imported energy, plus credit carried in. The **export ratio** is the exported ÷ imported energy of the last month PGE actually settled, because the inverter's own export counter cannot be used here (94.7 kWh against PGE's 342 for August).
- **Billable energy** = imported − credit, never below zero. Credit beyond this month's import is reported separately (`credit_left_kwh`), never as a negative bill.
- **Bill** = billable energy × the variable gross rate + the fixed gross fees, both taken from the lab's G11 tariff table: **1.0991 PLN/kWh** and **44.62 PLN** a month, last checked against a real invoice on **2026-09-27**.
- **Carried credit.** The connector reports left-over credit as fed-in kWh _before_ the factor, so it is counted as 0.8 × the left-over figure. It has been **0** in the one settled month on record (August 2026), so this reading is recorded as an assumption (`carried_credit_basis`) and is to be re-checked the first time it is not 0.
- **Confidence** follows the day count: **low** below **7** complete days, **medium** below **14**, **high** at 14 or more. It is forced down to **low** whenever the reference month lags (below), and whenever the closed-month check misses by more than **25%**.
- **No figure rather than a wrong one.** Instead of guessing, the forecast publishes `status: "no_data"` with a reason: `no_complete_days` (this month has no finished day yet, or the daily history could not be read), `settlement_facts_missing` (the factor, consumed or fed-in energy is missing, the settled month is under **50 kWh**, or the export ratio is above **2.0**), `rates_unavailable` (the tariff table could not be read). Everything reading the file is meant to check `status` before it reads a number — with one exception today: the deployed Telegram bot still renders `0.00 PLN` on a `no_data` body, until the bot backport lands (`bill-accuracy` 3.5, deferred).

**The reference month lags, and the figure re-bases when a new invoice arrives.** PGE issues an invoice about three weeks after the month it covers, and the connector only ever holds the latest one. So from the 1st of a month until roughly the 22nd, the newest settled month is the month _before_ last — and a month-old ratio can be far off: July's was 0.53 against August's 0.81, which on the same 549 kWh moves the estimate from 392 PLN to 258 PLN. The forecast records the gap as `reference_lag_months` (**0** when the reference is the month immediately before this one). Above 0 it widens the range, forces confidence to **low**, and ignores carried credit altogether, because the month in between has already drawn on that credit by an amount PGE has not reported yet. As soon as the new invoice arrives the forecast re-bases on it, so the figure can move noticeably around the 22nd of a month.

**The range.** Two errors are combined as independent (added in quadrature), not assumed to strike together: `u`, the sampling noise of a short month, is `max(15%, min(40%, 50 ÷ √days))`; `r`, how far the export ratio may have moved since it was measured, is **25%** at lag 0 and **40%** above it. The spread is `√((u × (billable + carried))² + (0.8 × ratio × r × imported)²)`, and the two ends are priced from billable − spread (never below zero) and billable + spread. On the real September data — 549 kWh imported, ratio 0.809, 15 complete days — that is **155–361 PLN around 258**.

**The closed-month check.** When the reference period carries an invoice total, the run re-prices that month from the consumed and fed-in energy PGE actually settled and compares the two (`closed_month_check`, `ok` within **±5%**). Without one the key is absent altogether, so anything reading the file treats it as optional. It tests the arithmetic, not the estimate: it cannot detect a wrong export ratio, and it drifts when PGE changes prices — August's implied rate was 1.14 PLN/kWh against today's 1.0991, which puts the check at −2.7% before any error of the formula (208.83 against the 214.66 invoiced). A failing check is always reported and never suppresses the projection.

Why credit the export at all: it is the whole difference between a plausible bill and a wrong one. Pricing every imported kWh put August at about 509 PLN against the 214.66 PLN actually invoiced; crediting it gives about 209 PLN, and July comes out at about 489 PLN against 495.22. Why estimate the export from last month's ratio instead of measuring it: PGE settled 342 kWh fed in for August where the inverter's counter read 94.7, and PGE records export at night, when the panels cannot be producing. Until that is explained (`context/changes/grid-export-mismatch/`) the house has no trustworthy in-month export figure of its own, and the last settled month's ratio is the closest honest stand-in — which is exactly why its age is published and why it drags the confidence down.

### What the app shows (S-07)

The app displays the lab's figure and never re-prices anything (`prd-v3.md:268`). Its own rules live in `src/lib/services/bill-forecast.ts`, each as a named constant, and they decide only whether a figure may be shown at all:

- **The range is the headline** ("od 155 zł do 360 zł"); the central estimate reads beneath it as "ok. 258 zł". Money is shown in whole złoty: a figure carrying a ±40% band quoted to the grosz would assert precision it does not have.
- **Freshness: 30 minutes**, judged against the body's own `generated_at`, never the push's `captured_at` — a stale forecast riding inside a fresh push is the documented failure, and `captured_at` staleness is already the live state card's job. Past it the card says how old the calculation is and shows no figure. The check applies to a `no_data` body as well, so a refusal kept for weeks is shown as stale rather than as its old reason.
- **Minimum 7 complete days**, counted from the `observed_days` list itself (at most 31 entries), not from the lab's `completed_days_used`, so a mismatched count cannot pass the gate. Below that the card is grey ("za mało danych") and quotes the average daily use instead of a złoty figure.
- **Verdict** against `closed_month_check.invoice_gross_pln`, the last real invoice: at or below it is good (compared in the whole złoty the card shows, so 214.90 against 214.66 is good, not "above 215 zł"), up to **+20%** is worth checking, above is a problem; exactly on a line takes the milder status. With no invoice to compare against there is no verdict at all — the badge says so — and never a fallback to the lab's own `computed_gross_pln`, which would change the colour's meaning from "against your last bill" to "against our own arithmetic".
- **Change against the last invoice (delta line).** Under the central estimate the card says how far it is from the reference invoice: "+43 zł (+20,1%) względem ostatniego rachunku za sierpień 2026". It is derived in `bill-forecast.ts` only after every refusal check above has passed, and only where the comparison is honest.
  - **Which invoice.** The one the verdict uses (`closed_month_check.invoice_gross_pln`), named from that check's own `period` (`closed_month_check.period`, not `settlement.reference_period`), with the year, so a two-month lag across a year end is unambiguous. With a lagging reference month it names that older month.
  - **Amount:** whole złoty on both sides, `round(central) − round(invoice)` with a real minus sign, so the two figures on the card subtract exactly. Zero is "bez zmian względem ostatniego rachunku za <miesiąc>".
  - **Percent:** `central ÷ invoice − 1`, rounded half away from zero to a whole percent, and left out when it rounds to 0 or the invoice rounds to 0 zł. **On the +20% verdict line it gets one decimal:** when the change is positive and its whole percent is 20 (so the whole 19,5 to 20,5% band, not only the line itself), the percent goes through `edgePercentLabel` (`src/lib/format/edge-percent.ts`), the same helper the usage card uses at ±15% and +40%. Exactly on the line it reads "+20,0%" (watch); past it at least "+20,1%" (problem), so the label and the badge always agree: 257,73 against 214,66 is "+43 zł (+20,1%)" and 240,6 against 200 is "+41 zł (+20,3%)", never "+20%" beside a "ponad 20% powyżej" badge. A decrease never triggers it (160 against 200 is "−40 zł (−20%)"). Everywhere else the percent is whole ("+60 zł (+30%)").
  - **Tone** comes from `verdictTone(central, invoice)`, the function the badge uses, so the delta line and the badge cannot disagree: good, watch or problem by the bands above. The arrow (up, down, flat), the sign and the words carry the meaning as well as the colour.
  - **Absent** (no delta line, the badge unchanged) when there is no invoice to compare with, when the forecast is for another month, and when the invoice's `period` cannot be read as a month (the label must name the invoice). It is never derived from `closed_month_check.diff_pct`: that figure is the check's own arithmetic drift (re-priced month against the invoice), not a change between periods.
- **Days in the estimate (bar).** "Policzone z pełnych dni 15 z 30" with a bar: the complete days counted (the same count as the minimum-days gate) over the number of days in the forecast's own month (its `month` key, else the Warsaw month now; 28 or 29 in February). The share fills the bar. It is withheld, keeping the period text, when the body claims more complete days than the month has (an inconsistent body, not a full bar). It does not depend on the invoice.
- **Plausibility ceiling: 7,000 PLN** on the central figure or either range end, derived from the lab's own bounds (200 kWh × 31 days × 1.0991 + 44.62 ≈ 6,859). Above it, and for a reversed range, a central estimate outside its own range, a negative settlement figure or a negative or non-finite derived kWh figure (projected import, credit, billable energy, credit left), the card shows the reason and no number. Shape and the sign of money are checked by the ingest contract; the sign of those kWh figures and of the settlement block is the card's job, so one lab glitch cannot 422 the push. A `generated_at` more than **5 minutes** ahead of the app's clock is a producer clock error and shows no number either. These guards are deliberately not in the ingest contract: a 422 rejects the whole push and would stop the live state and the recommendation too.
- **A forecast for another month is relabelled, not withheld** — the badge says which month it covers and the figure stays, as the recommendation card does for advice from an earlier day. A forecast generated at 23:58 on the last day of a month and read at 00:05 the next is still inside the freshness window.
- **The lab's `confidence` is shown as a nested badge**, and when the reference month lags its label names that month, so a lagging reference is disclosed instead of hiding the figure for three weeks of every month.
- **The closed-month check stays inside the "Na podstawie" block**, worded as a test of the arithmetic. Predicted-vs-actual reconciliation is a PRD non-goal (`prd-v3.md:278`).
- **Banked credit covering the whole month's import is said out loud**, because otherwise the card would show a strikingly low, trivially green figure (only the fixed fees) with no reason for it.

## Planned rules (PRD v3)

- **Seasonal norm for the ratings:** once about a year of history exists (July 2027), day and month ratings may compare with a same-season norm instead of the recent 14 days; until then every rating uses the recent norm ([Ocena dni i miesięcy](#ocena-dni-i-miesięcy)).
- **Consumption trends (S-20):** a remark when consumption rises or falls noticeably over weeks and months, against earlier periods (periods compared by their median, like the usage norm); against the same season a year before only once that history exists (about July 2027).
- **Texts for a non-expert (F-04, S-18):** a plain explanation of today and summaries of past days and months, written by the stronger model from the local model's observations, taking Polish seasons into account, without advice.
