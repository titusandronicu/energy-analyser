# Decisions

The main product and technical decisions, newest first, each with the reason. Detailed plans for each change are in `context/changes/` (active) and `context/archive/` (done); the product requirements are in `context/foundation/prd-v3.md` and the ordered work in `context/foundation/roadmap.md`.

## 2026-09-30

- **Completed days and months are rated good / neutral / bad by self-sufficiency against a recent 14-day norm, with a ±10 percentage-point band (S-17, `context/changes/period-ratings/`).** _Why:_ self-sufficiency (`1 − import ÷ use`) is bounded to 0–100% and higher is better, so a relative ±% band like the usage card's would mean different things at 30% and at 70%; absolute points read plainly ("27,4 punktu poniżej normy"), and exactly ±10 stays neutral by the same edge rule as the other verdicts.
  - **14 days, not 30:** the norm is the median of the qualifying days in the 14 days before the rated day, needing at least 7. It follows the autumn slide twice as fast as a 30-day window, which answers roadmap question 8 for the ratings; the usage card keeps its 30 days.
  - **The month is rated by the median of its rated days' gaps** from their own norms, with the same ±10 rule and at least 7 rated days, so it is immune to the seasonal slide that comparing one month with the previous one would suffer. The current month, today, future days and the quarter are not rated.
  - **Green / grey / red:** neutral uses the grey tone with its own word ("Przeciętny dzień"), because amber already means "warto sprawdzić"; "za mało danych" and "dane niespójne" are grey with their own words.
  - **Inconsistent days are skipped:** a day whose import exceeds its use (the early rows of roadmap question 7) reads "dane niespójne" and never enters a norm. Cloudy days are still rated, with a "Mało słońca" note when PV is under 70% of the norm days' PV, because self-sufficiency mostly follows the sun and the note gives the cause. The grid-import over-report from 4 August lowers all days alike, so it largely cancels in a comparison with the days just before.
  - **Placement:** the day view (under "Dzień w liczbach") and the month totals only, as the owner chose; not in the month grid, the quarter or the dashboard. The month's panel sits beside `TotalsPanel`, which the quarter also uses and which stays unrated.
  - **No seasonal norm yet:** same-season history won't exist before about July 2027, so every rating uses the recent norm and says so in its basis.

- **The history calendar (S-15) is day, month and quarter at `/dashboard/history`, with the period in the URL (`?day=`, `?month=`, `?quarter=`) and "Pulpit / Historia" links in the header; the year view stays in S-16.** _Why:_ history reaches back only to 16 July 2026 and the roadmap already splits the partial-year view out as S-16. A path under `/dashboard` is covered by the existing protected prefix, needs no middleware change, works with no JavaScript and can be bookmarked; an invalid or out-of-range period redirects to the default month. With no period the page opens on the current month from its 7th complete day and on the previous month before that, so it never opens on an almost empty "za mało danych" page early in a month. Totals are sums over complete days with their day count, reusing the 7-day minimum, and are never extrapolated (`context/changes/history-calendar/`).
  - **Left out:** the inverter's grid export (it under-reads and shows 0 from about mid-August while PGE recorded real export) and PGE figures (showing them needs an owner decision on privacy first; parked in the roadmap); both are explained in "Co to znaczy?". No hourly chart in the day view, because the app keeps hours for 35 days only, so days would behave differently. The ten missing and five empty days are not filled here: they stay gaps, and a separate lab change can re-send them from Home Assistant's hourly statistics with the `--from/--to` push (a roadmap follow-up).
  - **Shared page shell:** `AppShell.astro` (aurora background, header with the brand `h1` and the navigation, one `<main>`, an optional legend footer) is extracted from the dashboard's body so both pages share landmarks and layout; the dashboard's markup is otherwise unchanged and its 5-minute reload stays on the dashboard only, since past periods don't change.
  - **Historical recommendation view:** `toRecommendationView(row, now, { historical: true })` gives a past day's advice a neutral badge with its generation time, no stale flag and the findings' real colours, and the day view has its own "Rekomendacja z tego dnia" section instead of the dashboard card, which would call every past day's advice "dotyczy innego dnia" in red. The day shows its first recommendation from 06:00 (the one that set the battery plan) and folds the rest.
  - The invalid-period redirect is set on `Astro.response` rather than returned from the frontmatter, because a top-level `return` there crashes typescript-eslint's `no-misused-promises` rule.

- **The ten backfilled days (2026-07-16 to 2026-07-25) are kept as the lab sent them, including the four that don't balance (07-18, 07-21, 07-23, 07-24: PV + import and house use + export differ by 8–32 kWh).** _Why:_ the early PV and export counters are off, and the days already stored from the same period (such as 07-26 and 07-27) have the same gap under the same day rules; holding them back would take a new plausibility rule, which applies to every day and belongs in its own change (`history-backfill`, owner's decision after the dry run).

- **Hourly totals per clock hour may now leave the lab (`hourly_history`), for a "Godziny zużycia" card; this reverses S-10's "only hour-of-day aggregates may leave, never single readings or timestamps" for the inverter's own figures.** Still no sub-hour readings and no raw PGE rows leave the lab. _Why:_ the owner's real concern is how much the house draws from the grid at night, and which hours and days use the most and the least, and that needs one row per clock hour with its time. The source is the inverter, not the PGE hourly CSV (manual and a month behind), and each hour carries its reading count so the app can leave out gaps. The card ranks by house use, not grid draw: the inverter's grid reading agrees with PGE at night but over-reports daytime import since 4 August 2026, most likely a current-sensor placement or polarity fault to check on site, so daytime grid draw is shown beside each hour with a note that it may be overstated until the sensor is checked. The app keeps **35 days** of hours, and the lab sends only the last **48** complete hours each push (a few KB), which the app accumulates by upsert, because a whole-window list in every retained push would grow `ingest_pushes` to about 300 MB. The card rates nothing, so its badge is neutral and names the complete days its figures rest on (`context/changes/grid-export-mismatch/frame.md`).
  - _Correction (2026-09-30, after the production backfill):_ "agrees with PGE at night" overstated it. Over 26–31 August (22:00–06:00) the inverter read 11.8% above PGE's hourly-balanced import, single nights +5% to +21%; the card says "usually about 10–20% at night" (`docs/logic.md`, Godziny zużycia).

- **What to take from the lab's old analyser page and Home Assistant dashboards: nothing new enters M-1; the calendar's charts are hand-rolled SVG; F-04 keeps its facts apart from the narration; the rest is parked.** _Why:_ the `lab-feature-port` research (2026-09-28) mapped 24 candidates, and the cheapest wins have since shipped: the live power-flow visual, PV and export on the page, and relative-age freshness in the dashboard refresh, and the lab's bill forecast as S-07. With five weeks to the 2026-11-04 deadline the owner kept M-1's scope flat. S-15's day and month charts extend the sparklines' hand-rolled server-rendered SVG, following the entry below, rather than adding a chart library. F-04's new contract section carries its facts separately from the narration, because today a failed LLM call drops the whole recommendation block and every fact inside it; fixing it in the section F-04 adds anyway avoids a new item, and the old recommendation block keeps its gate. S-10 ships the FR-014 count only. Per-day battery totals (with battery charts and the energy-balance check), the explained anomaly list, a one-sentence flow summary, the battery-plan proposal and a visible micro-analysis feed are parked in the roadmap with their reasons; the micro-analysis feed stays input to the stronger model's explanations (FR-030) (`context/archive/2026-09-28-lab-feature-port/`).

## 2026-09-29

- **Every page supplies its own main landmark, and the dashboard's `<h1>` is the visible brand "Energy Analyser"; no skip link.** _Why:_ the dashboard refresh review (F10) found no main landmark and no `h1` on the dashboard, and no main on the sign-in and check-email pages. A single main in `Layout.astro` would have wrapped the dashboard's `<header>` and legend `<footer>` and dropped their banner and contentinfo roles, so the main sits in each page (`DashboardBody.astro` around the cards, the auth pages around their card) and the layout says so. The brand becomes the `h1` rather than a hidden heading because it already names the page, adds no copy and changes nothing visible; the card titles stay `h2`. A "Przejdź do treści" skip link was left out because the header holds one control. The CI smoke test checks that each of the three pages has a main landmark and an `h1` (`context/archive/2026-09-29-page-landmarks-headings/`).
- **The dashboard palette comes from the approved design (Claude Designer canvas "Energy Analyser dashboard refresh", saved in `context/archive/2026-09-29-dashboard-refresh-icons-sparklines/design/`), superseding the values PR 65 gave it; PV moves from violet to solar yellow, `#f5c462`, the same hex as the watch tone; and three extra tokens and a neutral tone join the set.** _Why:_ the owner approved the design, so its values are the source of truth and live once in `global.css` (`--background #0b1020`, `--card #151a29`, `--muted #1b2236`, `--muted-foreground #a9b1c6`, `--border #2a3350`, source hues `--flow-*`, tones `--tone-*`), with no hex in a component. The design uses three shades more than once and the owner confirmed them as tokens: `--inset #111627` (diagram stage and KPI row), `--hairline #232b45` (dividers, bar track) and `--border-strong #343e63` (control and logo borders); its remaining one-off shades map to the nearest existing token instead of new ones. `--tone-neutral #c3cadb` gives "cannot rate" its own tone, and each `--tone-*-surface` is an opaque `color-mix` 10% tint over the card, so no `/60` fade is needed. Yellow for PV reverses the earlier decision (live-flow entries below) to keep source colours away from the tone colours, and a PV node rated "watch" now shares its hue with PV's own. That is accepted because status never rests on colour: every chip carries a word and an icon, and the phone's tile corner glyph does too. Measured contrast is in `docs/logic.md` (Readability).
- **"Bilans systemu" is PV minus home load computed in the app from the snapshot's `state`, not a field the lab sends.** _Why:_ both inputs (`pv_w`, `home_load_w`) already sit in every push and the mapper already reads them, so the figure needs no contract change, no lab release and no new prerequisite, and it cannot disagree with the flows next to it. The rule is one place in `live-state.ts`: positive is a surplus, a difference under the 50 W noise floor is balanced and unsigned, a missing reading is a dash and never zero, and it is computed from unrounded watts, so it may differ by 0,1 kW from the difference of the two printed figures (said in `docs/logic.md`). It is not rated: a surplus or a shortfall is neither good nor bad, and a colour would claim a judgement the app cannot back. A lab field would have to be added, deployed and kept in step with two other readings for a subtraction.
- **Sparklines are hand-rolled server-rendered SVG with a pure path helper, no chart dependency; today is left out, missing days are gaps, three points is the minimum, and "Z PV wykorzystane" is omitted.** _Why:_ the series are 7 and 14 points, so a library would add weight for a 30-line function: the research measured uPlot at 545 KB (canvas, client only), Observable Plot at 1,5 MB (needs a DOM to render on the server), recharts at 7,4 MB (a client island) and `@visx/shape` at 227 KB (a dependency for a small function), and the earlier decision to add no chart dependency stands. `src/lib/sparkline.ts` (path and label) and `src/lib/services/daily-series.ts` (windows and gaps) are pure and unit-tested with exact strings; the component renders zero JavaScript and no animation, so nothing needs gating for reduced motion. Today's row is a running total that each push replaces, so plotting it would read as a collapse; the owner confirmed the previous 14 complete days and the usage chart's end on the compared day. A missing or null day is a gap, never a zero, and fewer than three points draws "za mało dni" (two points make a trend line out of nothing), while a failed history load says "historia niedostępna" so an error never reads as a short history. A flat series is drawn on the baseline (all zero) or at mid-height and always captioned "bez zmian, X kWh", because the export counter that feeds "Sprzedane do sieci dziś" under-reads (94,7 kWh against PGE's 342 for August), which makes a flat-zero series the likeliest outcome. For the same reason there is no "Z PV wykorzystane" row: it would be PV minus export and would overstate self-consumption. Revisit when the lab computes self-consumption.
- **The bill card names the invoice month in its delta line, takes the tone from the verdict function, and prints one decimal on the +20% line; a "days in the estimate" bar shows how much of the month the figure rests on.** _Why:_ "+43 zł" means nothing without saying against what, so the line names the invoice's month from `closed_month_check.period`, the period the invoice is actually for (`settlement.reference_period` is a different, lagging reference), and both figures are compared in the whole złoty the card shows so they subtract exactly. `closed_month_check.diff_pct` is not used: it is the check's own arithmetic drift (a re-priced month against its invoice, −2,7% for August), not a change between two periods, and presenting it as one would change what the number means. The line is absent without an invoice, for a forecast of another month or when the period cannot be read, rather than compare something else. A whole percent equal to 20 can sit next to either status (the approved sample is +20,06% and a problem, 240,6 against 200 is +20,3% and a problem), so the whole 19,5 to 20,5% band prints one decimal through `edgePercentLabel`, extracted from the usage card's `deltaLabel` and shared by both cards: a second copy of the rule would drift, and one implementation is tested once with exact strings. The tone comes from `verdictTone`, extracted from the badge's own comparison, so the line and the badge cannot disagree. The bar is the share of the forecast month's days behind the estimate ("15 z 30"), withheld when the body claims more days than the month has.
- **The bill card moves into the right column above the usage card, and the recommendation keeps two thirds; this supersedes the earlier entry that left the usage card alone on the right.** _Why:_ the approved artboards put the bill and usage cards in one column beside the recommendation, and the owner confirmed it when the plan was reviewed. It removes the full-width bill row, so the recommendation sits beside two short cards instead of one, and on the phone the order is live, recommendation, bill, usage. The move is layout only: no card's content, states or test ids changed with it.
- **Owner decision (2026-09-29): flow diagram rebuilt to the design, reversing the assumption that kept the verdict-tinted 2x2 squares.** _Why:_ that assumption was made before any render existed; after a first screenshot pass the owner saw that the four tinted squares with crossing straight connectors and 18 px values did not look like the approved artboards, and asked for the design. The diagram now has flat icon tiles in the source colours, PV alone on the left, a hub carrying the scale icon with "Bilans systemu" and its value under it, and Home, Battery and Grid in one column on the right. The connectors are dashed S-curves from the hub's horizontal edge to each node's vertical middle, each with a chevron at its end, so the tangent at the hub is always horizontal and the arrowhead always level (`flow-geometry.ts`, rewritten and tested with exact paths). Verdicts are shown as a chip under the value (grid gets a neutral "Bez oceny" constant) and a tone-tinted tile border, instead of a tinted square, which keeps the owner's earlier wish that quality be visible on the node without a card behind it. The phone shows only tile, short label and value; the chips and detail lines stay reachable in the selected-node strip and in "Odczyty", a rated tile carries a small tone glyph on its corner so the verdict is never colour alone, and the accessible name of every node is unchanged. Two more pieces of the design come with it: a stale snapshot dims the balance to `opacity-70` (about 4,6:1; `opacity-45` measured 2,6:1), and its connectors dim to `opacity-65` (3,1 to 6,1:1 on the stage; `opacity-45` gave 2,1 to 3,6:1 and was raised after the implementation review), and the Schemat/Odczyty/Wstrzymaj controls move into the title row beside the heading and badge. Small fidelity tweaks ride along: the bill range at 30 px in the primary colour, the recommendation heading at 22 px, and "Pokaż szczegóły" with a primary-tinted outline. LiveFlow's behaviour is unchanged: the motion gate (fresh, at least 50 W, not paused), pause, the direction rules, the verdict rules and colours and the keyboard and `aria` contract; the direction, idle, stale and paused rules moved into a pure `flow-connector-state.ts` with exact tests, because they were untested inside the component and the rebuild touched every line that read them.
- **The derived kWh figures of the bill forecast are sign-checked in the card's mapper, not in the contract.** _Why:_ the lab has published a negative `reference_feed_in_kwh`, which gives a negative export ratio, and credit is 0.8 × export ratio × import, so `projected_credit_kwh` (and through it `projected_billable_kwh` and `credit_left_kwh`) can go negative too. Declared `nonNegative`, one such figure would 422 the whole push and stop the live state and the recommendation, the exact outcome the settlement block was left permissive to avoid. `projected_import_kwh`, `projected_credit_kwh`, `projected_billable_kwh` and `credit_left_kwh` are now bare `z.number()` and a negative or non-finite one blanks this card alone ("błędne dane w wyliczeniu"); money, range, pricing, the observed days and the daily average stay `nonNegative`. The alternative, relying on the lab to clamp these fields, rests on a script the app does not control.
- **The bill forecast contract accepts the lab's own output as it is written, instead of the lab reshaping its file.** _Why:_ the preflight parse of the real `current-month-bill-forecast.json` against the deployed strict contract found four mismatches that would each have 422'd the whole push: the settled period is the connector's text ("01.08.2026 - 31.08.2026") rather than "2026-08" (in `settlement.reference_period` and `closed_month_check.period`), `settlement.factor_implied` is a number or null rather than a boolean, and the file carries a `privacy` notice the contract did not declare. All four are widenings (the month form, a boolean and a missing `privacy` stay valid), so an older sender is unaffected, and the app names the month a period ends in, as the lab's own lag calculation does. The alternative, a mapping step in the lab's push script, would have let the lab and the contract drift apart again on the next field; the section is sent unchanged and `scripts/fixtures/bill-forecast/lab-shape.json` (a synthetic file with the real file's shape and none of its values) is parsed in `npm test` to keep it that way. The contract change has to be in production before the lab sends the section.
- **The recommendation card shows the lab's findings as a new block, "Najważniejsze ustalenia", not as a return of an earlier one.** _Why:_ the research found that no always-visible findings block ever existed in the repository; findings were only bare fact strings under "Na podstawie", inside a collapsed `<details>` and later behind the disclosure button. Treating the work as a revert would have restored the wrong thing. The block is new UI built on the lab's `local_findings` (title, fact, severity): the card shows the first five with a severity chip and keeps meaning, suggested check and the rest behind the disclosure, so the reader sees what the advice rests on without opening anything.
- **Finding severity maps to a chip word without guessed categories, and chips on stale cards are neutral.** _Why:_ `warn`, `ok` and `info` become "Warto sprawdzić", "Dobrze" and "Informacja", and anything else "Bez oceny"; a category or icon per finding would mean guessing a kind from free text, so none is shown (a lab `kind` field would be a separate change). On a stale card (over two hours old or from an earlier day) every chip stays grey but keeps its word: a green "Dobrze" beside a "sprzed 3 godz." badge would say the finding is still true. The mapper decides this once, so the card holds no freshness logic.
- **The advice lead is the leading headings plus the first block, a one-line intro ending in ":" pulls in the list after it, and the lead is not capped.** _Why:_ the parser splits "Zalecenia na dziś:" and its bullets into two blocks, so "first block only" would show an intro and hide the actions it introduces. Capping the lead in characters would cut model text mid-sentence or mid-list with no honest place to cut, and the app never edits the advice; a long lead is the lab's choice to fix, not the card's to trim. Without a list after the colon line the intro stands alone.
- **`ForecastCard` is removed and the forecast is inlined into the recommendation card; the dashboard keeps its 2/3 + 1/3 grid with the usage card alone in the right column.** _Why:_ the forecast is generated with the advice and shares its age, so a separate card could look current next to stale advice; inside the card it sits under the same status badge and takes the date-only labelling from `isFromEarlierDay`. The Phase 3 screenshots (1440 and 390 px) showed the page balanced with the usage card top-aligned beside the taller recommendation, and no overflow at 390 px, so no layout change was needed beyond deleting the card.
- **Motion is for a current recommendation only, CSS-only, and the reduced-motion override is unconditional; the data panel stays opaque.** _Why:_ movement should mean "this is fresh", so a stale, watch or problem card never moves (the same rule as the live flow diagram). Only data sections animate (findings, forecast, the disclosure fade), never the advice text, so reading is not delayed. Two short one-shot animations (an entrance and a fade) in `global.css`, used in three places, need no JavaScript, and one `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` rule switches them off, next to the flow-dash rule; the chevron's rotation is `motion-safe:` for the same reason. The panel keeps its opaque card surface: the design brief limits the glass and blur to the header, and data panels stay opaque so text contrast does not depend on what sits behind them.
- **The dashboard palette is "Frosted Aurora": deep blue-black background, navy panels, a restrained aurora wash behind opaque data panels, glass on the header only.** _Why:_ the owner approved that direction (`context/archive/2026-09-29-frosted-aurora-dashboard/`). `--background` `#08111f`, `--card`/`--popover` `#111827`, `--muted` `#18223a`, `--muted-foreground` `#b8c4d9` and `--border` `#27344f` replace the hex values in the earlier glass-restyle design brief, whose rules still hold: data panels are opaque (no blur, no translucency), the header is a mostly opaque card colour with 16 px blur and a solid fallback, and the aurora is a static background whose colour blobs sit behind `@supports (color: color-mix(...))` so older browsers keep a plain gradient. The four older dashboard cards, `TermsExplained` and the header moved from `text-blue-100` / `bg-white/5` literals to the semantic tokens; no field, state, copy or test id changed, and measured text and chip contrast on the panel, muted and header surfaces stays at or above 6:1. Other pages (`auth/*`, `LibBadge`, `button`) keep their literals.
- **Owners can read `daily_energy.captured_at` (column grant, migration `20260929101548_owner_read_daily_energy_captured_at.sql`), and the consumption verdict is not rated when today's row is more than 15 minutes older than the snapshot.** _Why:_ `daily_history` is optional in a push, so a fresh state-only push leaves an older daily total in place, and comparing that total with the norm at the new capture time gave a false "good" (5 kWh at 06:00 read as consumption to 12:00). The client could not see the row's age, so the timestamp is exposed under the existing owner-only policy; `updated_at` and `push_id` stay unreadable. The migration is applied in production with the deploy; without it the consumption chip reads "Bez oceny · brak czasu historii" and the rest of the dashboard is unaffected.
- **Live-flow motion and arrows are gated on freshness: a flow moves only when the snapshot is at most 15 minutes old, the reading is at least 50 W and the user has not paused; arrowheads always show direction.** _Why:_ the old pulse ran on watts alone, so a three-hour-old snapshot still looked alive, the exact failure the design brief forbids. Motion now means "this is happening now", and a static arrowhead still says which way the energy goes when motion is off, paused or reduced by the system.
- **The battery, PV and consumption squares carry a verdict with these edges: battery good from 30% and problem under 10%; PV rated only from 15:00 Warsaw, good from 85% and problem under 60% of the expected share; consumption rated from 06:00, good up to +15% over the pro-rated norm and problem above +40%. Grid is not rated.** _Why:_ the owner chose the widest option on the mockup, and every rule reuses data and bands that already exist: the battery's 30% and 10% are the icon bands, the consumption edges are the usage card's, the PV edges are new and proposed. PV is judged from 15:00 because earlier in the day partial totals say little about the day; grid import and export are neither good nor bad. The verdicts read the capture time, not the clock, and a stale or missing reading is never rated ("bez oceny" with the reason).
- **The share of the day's PV expected by capture time comes from a 12-month table estimated from clear-sky solar geometry, rising linearly from 15:00 to the hour the day is done.** _Why:_ the share produced by 15:00 varies from 0.73 in June and July to 1.00 in December at this latitude, so one constant would mislabel whole seasons. The table is an estimate for a flat array, not a measurement; it is to be revisited after a year of lab data.
- **`StatusBadge` and the verdict chips share the tone tokens (`--tone-*`).** _Why:_ a badge in a card header and a chip on a square say the same thing with the same words, so they use one palette; it removes the emerald/amber/red literals from the badge, and contrast is measured once per tone (4.5:1) instead of per component.
- **The existing Radix-based `Button` is kept and extended instead of migrating to Base UI, which the design brief asked for.** _Why:_ the auth forms and the disclosure button already use it; a second button primitive, or a migration, for two toggles would be more surface than the change is worth. The deviation is recorded here so it is a decision, not drift.
- **The intraday history chart is deferred; the missing contract is intraday interval readings in kW pushed by the lab.** _Why:_ the contract carries only daily totals (`daily_history`), which cannot be turned into a power-over-time chart, and real history only starts in July 2026. Daily totals belong to the period selector, not to a kW chart. No chart dependency was added.
- **`localStorage` is the first client-side persistence, holding only the diagram/readings view and the pause flag, never a reading.** _Why:_ the dashboard reloads itself every five minutes, which would otherwise reset the owner's choices. Every access is guarded, the server render always uses the defaults so it matches the first client render, and nothing sensitive or stale can be stored because no data is.

## 2026-09-28

- **The bill forecast card leads with the range, not the central figure.** _Why:_ US-03 asks for "a range rather than a single exact figure", and this range is wide on purpose — 155–361 PLN around 258, roughly ±40% — because it combines the sampling noise of a short month with how far the export ratio may have moved since it was measured. A single headline number would read as a promise the estimate cannot keep; the central estimate stays, one line below, as "ok. 258 zł".
- **A lagging reference month is disclosed on the card, not hidden behind a grey state.** _Why:_ PGE invoices about three weeks late and the connector holds only the latest invoice, so from the 1st of a month until roughly the 22nd the newest settled month is the month _before_ last. Withholding the figure whenever `reference_lag_months > 0` would leave the card blank for most of its life and fail US-03. Instead the confidence badge names the month the settlement actually comes from, and the "Na podstawie" block says how far behind it is and why.
- **The colour verdict compares the projection with the last real invoice, and there is no verdict without one.** _Why:_ "is this bill going to be bad?" only means something against a bill the owner actually paid. Bands are at or below the invoice (good), up to +20% (watch) and above (problem), with exactly on a line taking the milder status, as everywhere else in the app. Falling back to the lab's own `closed_month_check.computed_gross_pln` was rejected: it would silently change the colour's meaning from "against your last bill" to "against our own arithmetic", so the badge says there is nothing to compare with instead.
- **The card judges freshness on the forecast's own `generated_at`, 30 minutes, not on the push's `captured_at`.** _Why:_ the two clocks answer different questions, and the documented failure is precisely a stale forecast riding inside a perfectly fresh push — on a write failure nginx kept serving the previous file, still `status: "ok"`, with the figure 2.4x overstated. The lab recomputes with every 5-minute push, so half an hour means it has stopped computing. `captured_at` staleness is already visible on the live state card.
- **Validation is split: shape and sign in the ingest contract, plausibility in the view model.** _Why:_ the contract is strict and a zod failure 422s the _whole_ payload, so one bad cost field would stop the live state and the recommendation too. Anything that is a publisher bug rather than a malformed payload — a range end above 7,000 PLN, a reversed range, a negative settlement figure (a negative `feed_in_kwh` once produced a 41M-PLN range end) — therefore blanks this one card and leaves the rest of the dashboard updating.
- **The closed-month check is disclosed inside "Na podstawie" only, worded as a test of the arithmetic.** _Why:_ predicted-vs-actual reconciliation is a PRD non-goal, and the check cannot do that job anyway: it re-prices a settled month from what PGE itself settled, so it cannot detect a wrong export ratio, and it drifts when PGE changes prices (−2.7% for August before any error of the formula). Surfacing it as an accuracy figure would claim more than it measures; hiding it entirely would leave the card's one honest self-check unpublished.

## 2026-09-27

- **The house is settled by net-metering ("opust") with a factor of 0.8, not net-billing; the lab's bill forecast credits exported energy instead of pricing every imported kWh.** _Why:_ the forecast read 610 PLN for September against a real August invoice of 214.66, and the cause was the settlement model, not the rates or the arithmetic. The PGE connector states the factor outright, and the credited formula reproduces August within 3% (≈209 PLN) and July within about 1% (≈489 against 495.22), where pricing gross import gives ≈509 and ≈816. The net-billing wording in the lab's Home Assistant report prompt was corrected at the same time.
- **The Home Assistant PGE connector is the billing source for closed periods.** _Why:_ it is the only source that states what PGE itself settled — consumed and fed-in energy, the 0.8 factor, the credit carried forward, the invoice total and the billing period — so the forecast is checked against the invoice on every run instead of against the inverter. It carries no hourly or daily data (it reads the mBOK sales API every 8 h, latest invoice only), so within the month the daily import still comes from the inverter. Its friendly names and balance attributes hold the account e-mail, PPE and invoice number, so the lab copies values only.
- **In-month export is estimated from the last settled month's export/import ratio, and the estimate says how old that ratio is.** _Why:_ the credit needs an export figure and the inverter cannot give one (94.7 kWh against PGE's 342 for August). PGE issues an invoice about three weeks after the month ends, so for most of a month the newest ratio is a month too old, and ratios move sharply between months (July 0.53, August 0.81 — 392 PLN against 258 on the same import). Rather than hide that, the forecast publishes the lag, widens the range, drops to low confidence and re-bases when the new invoice lands.
- **Meter-grade live data over wM-Bus is parked as future development.** _Why:_ reading import and export straight from the PGE smart meter would remove the estimate altogether, but it needs the meter model checked, a request to PGE Dystrybucja to enable the interface and issue the key, and an 868 MHz receiver near the meter — too much for a change whose ratio estimate already reproduces the invoices. MojeIRE (CSIRE) 15-minute data is the official alternative once its consumer API is published.
- **Why PGE records export the inverter never sees goes to a change of its own (`grid-export-mismatch`).** _Why:_ it is a measurement question, not a billing one, and the bill forecast works without the answer. Bundling an open hardware investigation into the forecast would have blocked a figure that is already 3% accurate; the evidence is handed over instead.
